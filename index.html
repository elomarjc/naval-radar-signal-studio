🚢 Maritime Radar Signal Processing Studio

🎯 The Industrial Problem Solved

Naval surveillance radars must detect small, fast-moving surface threats (stealth craft, RHIBs) amidst severe non-Gaussian ocean sea clutter (Weibull/K-distribution) and land mass reflections. Standard fixed-threshold systems trigger thousands of false alarms, while standard Cell-Averaging CFAR suffers from target masking—when two vessels are close, the larger vessel raises the detection threshold, blinding the radar to the smaller craft.

💡 How This Digital Twin Solves It

Implements a complete naval radar signal chain: 360° Plan Position Indicator (PPI) with phosphor persistence decay, A-Scope oscilloscope, statistical sea clutter generators, 3-Pulse Moving Target Indication (MTI) Doppler filter (canceling stationary clutter), and Ordered-Statistic CFAR (OS-CFAR) to prevent target masking.

📊 Telemetry & Control Glossary

  • OS-CFAR (Ordered-Statistic): Adaptive detection algorithm that sorts reference window cells and selects the k-th rank, preventing close adjacent targets from masking each other.
  • MTI Doppler Filter: Pulse-to-pulse delay line canceler [H(z) = (1 - z^-1)^2] that rejects DC and stationary ground/sea clutter while passing Doppler-shifted moving targets.
  • PPI Scope (Plan Position Indicator): Polar radar display mapping range and bearing with phosphor persistence decay.
